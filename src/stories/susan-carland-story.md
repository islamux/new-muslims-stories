## My Life Before Islam

Susan Carland grew up in Melbourne, raised in the Baptist and Uniting churches — a Christian upbringing she still speaks of with warmth and enthusiasm. She attended public schools, and from the age of seven one of her favourite pastimes was ballet. Hers was a settled, busy childhood of family, faith, and dance. She went on to study at Monash University, and it was there, in her very first year, that the familiar world of her upbringing began to open onto something larger.

## The Path to Islam

Her interest in Islam did not arrive in a single flash. It grew steadily, through reading and reflection, until the questions she had been carrying found a direction. What drew her was not a rejection of faith but its deepening: the same instinct that had made her a thoughtful Christian girl now led her to look more closely at the religion she was encountering. She became increasingly interested in Islam during that first year of university, and the interest did not fade.

## The Moment of Guidance

At the age of nineteen, in her first year at Monash, Susan Carland embraced Sunni Islam. It was a decision made in the middle of an ordinary student life — not in crisis, but in conviction. The girl who had grown up in the Baptist and Uniting churches chose a path her family had not walked, and she has always spoken of her Christian upbringing with affection rather than bitterness, holding it as part of who she is rather than something to be discarded.

## Legacy

Carland became one of Australia's most recognisable Muslim voices. She teaches gender studies, politics, and sociology at Monash University, with a special focus on Muslim women and on Muslims in Australia. Her doctorate, completed in 2015, examined how Muslim women fight sexism from within their own communities and traditions, from a pro-faith perspective, and in 2017 she published it as the book Fighting Hislam: Women, Faith and Sexism. She was a founding member and presenter of the SBS comedy talk show Salam Cafe, is active in the Islamic Council of Victoria, and hosted the Australian version of Child Genius in 2018. In 2002 she married the lawyer and broadcaster Waleed Aly; they have two children and live in Melbourne. Her life shows that faith can be inherited, questioned, and then chosen — and that a woman can be entirely at home in Islam while refusing to leave her critical mind at the door.
