## My Life Before Islam

Mahmoud Abdul-Rauf was born Chris Jackson on March 9, 1969, in Gulfport, Mississippi. Raised by a single mother in poverty, he grew up with his two brothers in a household where food was sometimes scarce, and he entered fourth grade late and spent time in special education. He had an undiagnosed condition that caused uncontrollable movements and sounds, eventually identified as Tourette syndrome, which made school painful. Basketball became his refuge, and his extraordinary shooting discipline, during one practice he made nearly three hundred consecutive free throws, turned him into one of the most gifted guards in the country. He starred at Louisiana State University and was selected third overall in the 1990 NBA Draft.

## The Path to Islam

During his years at LSU, his coach Dale Brown gave him a copy of "The Autobiography of Malcolm X", which opened his eyes to the history of racial injustice in America and to the teachings of Islam. During his rookie season with the Denver Nuggets he was introduced to the Qur'an and began to study it intently, later recalling that Islam offered answers to questions he could not find anywhere else. He found in the faith a discipline and a spiritual depth that matched the discipline he had always poured into his game. He converted during the early 1990s and in 1993 officially changed his name from Chris Jackson to Mahmoud Abdul-Rauf, a name holding praise of God and His mercy.

## The Moment of Guidance

Abdul-Rauf's faith soon began to shape how he lived. In the 1995-96 season he was having one of the best years of his career, having led the league in free-throw percentage, when he made a decision that changed everything: he refused to stand for the American national anthem before games, explaining that a flag that symbolized so much injustice and oppression could not receive his salute. The NBA suspended him, and a compromise was reached allowing him to stand during the anthem with his head bowed, silently reciting prayers for those who suffer. He said his conscience as a Muslim simply would not allow him to take part in what he called a nationalistic ritual.

## Legacy

Abdul-Rauf's protest, which cost him dearly and shortened his time in the NBA, became a landmark moment in the history of athlete activism. Decades later, when NFL quarterback Colin Kaepernick knelt during the anthem in 2016, players and journalists recognized Abdul-Rauf as the pioneer of that stand, and he has spoken widely about faith, racial justice, and mental health. He continued his career in leagues around the world and remains an outspoken advocate for the oppressed. His life stands as a powerful example of a man who placed his faith and his conscience above fame and financial security, and who used his gifts in the service of justice.
