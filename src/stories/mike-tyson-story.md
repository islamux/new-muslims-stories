## My Life Before Islam

Mike Tyson was born on June 30, 1966, in Brooklyn, New York, and grew up in a neighborhood of poverty and violence where he was often in trouble as a youth. Boxing saved him, and under the guidance of trainer Cus D'Amato he rose with astonishing speed, becoming the youngest heavyweight champion in history in November 1986 when he knocked out Trevor Berbick at the age of twenty. He dominated his division and unified the heavyweight title in 1987, a figure of extraordinary power and promise. But away from the ring, fame and turmoil pulled him in another direction, and in February 1990 he lost his title in a stunning upset that opened a turbulent period in his life.

## The Path to Islam

Raised Catholic, Tyson had been baptized as a Baptist in 1988, but a deeper and more lasting change came through Islam. He has said that he embraced Islam before he entered prison in 1992. Inside the Indiana Youth Center he attended Islamic prayer services and study sessions with fellow inmates, and he recalled that the faith gave him a discipline and a sense of purpose he had never known. He studied the religion seriously, joined the prison's Muslim community, and found in Islam a framework for understanding his own life and its struggles. His conversion became widely known during those years, and in fact he said later that he never felt the need to change his name.

## The Moment of Guidance

In 1992, while serving his prison sentence in Indiana, Mike Tyson's conversion to Islam was reported around the world, and his study of the religion became one of the most talked-about stories in sports. He attended Islamic services, took classes, and observed the discipline of prayer and fasting alongside the prison's small Muslim community. On the morning of his release in March 1995, he went first not to a press conference but to a mosque, where he knelt in prayer with Muhammad Ali beside him, an image that moved observers everywhere. He has said that Islam gave him the inner peace and the sense of accountability that he had long been missing, and it has remained a defining presence in his life ever since.

## Legacy

Mike Tyson's conversion is among the most widely known in the history of American sport, and his story introduced Islam to millions of people who had never encountered it before. After his release from prison he regained the heavyweight championship in 1996, joining a short list of boxers who have won the title twice, and he remained one of the most recognizable figures in sport for three decades. He has spoken candidly about the role of faith in his life, and his journey from the depths of prison to a public embrace of Islam has been an inspiration to many Muslims, particularly in the world of professional sport. In 2024 his fight with Jake Paul once again broke attendance records, a reminder of the enduring fascination with his remarkable story.
