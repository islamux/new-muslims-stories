## My Life Before Islam

Wayne Dillon Parnell was born on 30 July 1989 in Port Elizabeth, in South Africa's Eastern Cape, and grew up in the cricket culture of that city. He attended Grey High School, leaving in 2007, and went on to study human resource management at Nelson Mandela Metropolitan University. A left-arm fast bowler, he rose quickly: he played for Eastern Province, then the Warriors, and by 2009 he was in the national side, making his one-day and Twenty20 debuts against Australia and his Test debut against England in January 2010.

## The Path to Islam

Parnell's interest in Islam grew quietly, through his own reading, during the years when his career was taking off. He has described a period of personal study rather than a single dramatic encounter, and he has been careful to say that his teammates — among them the Muslim players Hashim Amla and Imran Tahir — and the team manager Mohammad Moosajee had no influence on his decision. The choice was his own.

## The Moment of Guidance

In January 2011, at twenty-one, Parnell became a Muslim. He kept the matter private for months and announced it publicly only on 30 July 2011 — his twenty-second birthday. He considered taking the name Waleed, which he understood to mean "newborn son", but in the end he continued to be known simply as Wayne. The name stayed the same; his life did not.

## Legacy

Parnell went on to represent South Africa across all three formats — Test, one-day and Twenty20 cricket — and became one of the relatively few Muslim players in the international game. He has appeared in advertising for PETA, the animal-rights organisation, and has spoken about his faith when asked, without turning it into a performance. For a young sportsman in a country where religion and identity are never far from the surface, his quiet, considered conversion remains a striking example of a decision made by heart and mind together.
