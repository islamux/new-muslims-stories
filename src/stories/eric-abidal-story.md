## My Life Before Islam

Éric Abidal was born on September 11, 1979, in Saint-Genis-Laval near Lyon, France, into a family of Martiniquais origin. Raised as a Catholic by his parents, he grew up in modest circumstances, and his natural athleticism drew him to football from an early age. He began his professional career at Lyon before moving to Lille, where his performances as a strong, attacking full-back earned him a place in the France national team. Yet despite his rising fame, Abidal has always spoken of a deeper emptiness before his conversion, a life that was successful on the surface but restless underneath, a search for meaning that football alone could not satisfy.

## The Path to Islam

Abidal's path to Islam began through quiet reflection and encounters with Muslim friends and teammates, and he has described his conversion as coming without any pressure from anyone. In his twenties, while at the peak of his playing career, he began to read about the religion, drawn in particular to its simplicity and to the idea of submitting to the will of one God. He embraced Islam and took the name Bilal, honouring the Ethiopian companion of the Prophet. His wife, Hayet, remained a supportive presence, and the faith he adopted gradually became a central pillar of his life, giving a new centre to both his character and his home.

## The Moment of Guidance

In 2007, in the middle of his career and surrounded by the glitter of European football, Éric Abidal became a Muslim and took the name Bilal. He later described the moment as a gift that suddenly appeared in his life, adding that he embraced Islam with full confidence, certain in his heart that it was the truth. The decision did not define him by outward display, but it transformed him from within. Years afterward, when illness struck and he faced the fight of his life, Abidal would repeatedly credit his faith with giving him the strength to survive, describing how prayer and trust in God carried him through the darkest days of his existence.

## Legacy

Eric Abidal's legacy is written in both sporting triumph and personal courage. He won league titles at Lyon, Barcelona and Monaco, and lifted two Champions League trophies, in 2009 and 2011. In 2011, just weeks after surgery for a tumour on his liver, he played the entire Champions League final at Wembley, and in April 2012 he received a liver transplant, returning to the pitch the following year. Through illness and recovery, he became a symbol of resilience, and he has spoken openly of how Islam and the dua of well-wishers sustained him. He has continued in football as a sporting director, and his story of faith and survival remains an inspiration across France and the Arab world.
