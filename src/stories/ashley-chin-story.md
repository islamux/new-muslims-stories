## My Life Before Islam

Ashley Anthony Chin was born on August 21, 1982, in Gypsy Hill in south London, to parents of Jamaican descent. Raised as a Christian by his mother, Andrea Ellis, and his elder sister, Kareena, he grew up on a council estate in South London, where he developed a talent for poetry and acting but also became caught up in gang culture, associated with a street collective known as South Man Syndicate, where he was called Smalls. He began acting professionally in 1999, appearing in the BBC film "Storm Damage" and making his stage debut at the Royal Court Theatre, yet behind the successes lay a restless search for meaning and a life he feared he could not escape.

## The Path to Islam

Recalling his own search later, Chin told interviewers that he had asked himself why he existed and what his life was for, questions the streets could not answer. In 2002, at the age of nineteen, he converted to Islam, adopting the name Muslim Belal and stepping away from his career for a time to focus on learning his new religion. He travelled to Egypt in 2004 and lived there for a year to study the faith, an experience that deepened his understanding far beyond the surface practice he had seen. His mother and his elder sister later followed him into Islam, and his conversion slowly reshaped the direction of his life and work.

## The Moment of Guidance

In 2002, the turning point of his life came when he embraced Islam and took the name Muslim Belal. The young man who had once moved as Smalls through the gangs of South London turned instead to prayer, remembrance and a new identity grounded in faith. When he returned to music and acting, he carried that identity with him, performing at the Global Peace and Unity event in 2005 and releasing his debut album "Pray Hard" in 2009, whose themes of repentance and seeking Allah grew directly out of his own experience. For Ashley Chin, the moment of guidance was not only a change of creed but a new direction for his whole life.

## Legacy

Ashley Chin built a lasting career as an actor, rapper and screenwriter, starring in films such as "Victim," "Anuvahood" and "The Intent," while releasing albums including "The Transition" and "My Sumaya." He has used his poetry and his testimony to speak to young people, delivering workshops and talks at schools, universities and youth clubs, and he works as a life coach. His autobiography "Faith," published in October 2019, tells openly of his journey from gang life to Islam, and he has written and directed a feature film based on the book. His story remains one of the most moving examples of a British artist rebuilt by faith.
