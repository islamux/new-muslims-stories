## My Life Before Islam

William Webb was born on June 29, 1972, in Oklahoma, into a Christian family in which his grandfather served as a preacher. His family attended a local Church of Christ twice a week throughout his childhood, and birthdays were marked by the gift of a Bible with his name on it. Even as a boy, however, he questioned some of the beliefs he was taught, including the Trinity. At fourteen he lost interest in religion altogether and passed through what he later described as a spiritual crisis. Music filled the gap: he became a hip-hop DJ and producer, making records with local artists, and at seventeen he joined a neighbourhood gang. By his own account his involvement was limited, though it included a week in jail at nineteen for stealing hubcaps.

## The Path to Islam

He was nineteen, selling music tapes at a swap meet, when he met a Muslim man selling incense and handing out copies of the Qur'an. Webb took one home and read it in secret for several months. The book did not strike him as a foreign document; it spoke about God, responsibility, and the human heart in a way that answered questions his earlier religious education had left open. The more he read, the more convinced he became that the Qur'an was not the work of a man.

## The Moment of Guidance

He converted in 1992, during his freshman year at the University of Central Oklahoma, and took the name Suhaib. That Thanksgiving he broke the news to his parents over dinner, a meal at which his mother had cooked both a turkey and a ham, the latter forbidden in Islam. His mother, Mary Lynn Webb, was not happy at the time, though she later said she was proud of the man he had become. Webb left his career as a DJ, graduated with a degree in education, and began studying Islam privately under a Senegalese sheikh.

## Legacy

He went on to study Islamic law at Al-Azhar University in Cairo, where he also served as head of translation at Dar al-Iftaa, Egypt's principal fatwa office. He returned to the United States and became imam of the Islamic Society of Greater Oklahoma City, then moved to California before being inaugurated in 2011 as imam of the Islamic Society of Boston Cultural Center, the largest Islamic centre in New England. He later served as resident scholar at the Islamic Center of New York University and founded the Suhaib Webb Institute for Sacred Studies. Named among the world's 500 most influential Muslims, he is known for an American-inflected Islam that speaks to young people in the language of their own culture.
