## My Life Before Islam

Mahershala Ali was born in Oakland, California, on 16 February 1974, and was given the name Mahershalalhashbaz Gilmore — taken from the Book of Isaiah in the Bible, the longest personal name in the Book. He was raised in Hayward by a remarkable line of Baptist women: his mother was an ordained minister, and her own mother had been a minister before her. The church was the center of his childhood, while his father left for a career dancing on Broadway and died in 1994. His first ambition was basketball: he won a scholarship to Saint Mary's College of California, playing under the name Hershal Gilmore, and became the first in his family to graduate from university. Yet his love of sport faded as he saw how the athletes around him were treated, and a small part in a campus play opened a different door — acting.

## The Path to Islam

Ali moved to New York and entered the graduate acting program at NYU, completing his master's in 2000. In those years away from the church of his childhood, he describes a search that turned into a spiritual awakening. The answers he had been looking for about God, purpose, and how to live were, to him, found most clearly in Islam. In 2000 he declared his shahada and became a Muslim, taking a new surname: Ali. The next year he joined the Ahmadiyya Muslim Community, deepening his study of his new faith.

## The Moment of Guidance

Becoming Mahershala Ali — the new surname resting on a sacred text — marked a complete redrawing of his identity. It was not without cost. After the attacks of 11 September 2001, he was repeatedly profiled at airports and banks as a Muslim man, yet he has said such prejudice was "not a shock" for a Black man in America. What being Muslim gave him, he has explained, was discipline and stillness — an anchor of prayer and purpose in a profession famous for its turbulence.

## Legacy

In 2017 he won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actor for Moonlight — widely reported as the first Muslim actor to win an acting Oscar — and in 2019 he won his second, for Green Book. The New York Times has since named him among the greatest actors of the twenty-first century, and Time listed him among the world's most influential people. He uses his platform, too: he signed the Artists4Ceasefire letter during the Gaza war and spoke for the safety of its people. His journey, from a pew in an Oakland church to the world stage, shows how a searching heart can find peace in a faith it did not inherit.
