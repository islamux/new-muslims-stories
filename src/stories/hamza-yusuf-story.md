## My Life Before Islam

Hamza Yusuf was born Mark Hanson in 1958 in Walla Walla, Washington, the son of two academics teaching at Whitman College, and grew up in northern California. His family was Christian but not of one piece: his father was a Catholic of Irish descent, while his mother — half Greek, half Irish — was Greek Orthodox, and it was in his mother's church that he was raised. A clever, restless student, he attended preparatory schools on both the East and West coasts. By his late teens he was searching, and not especially within the faith of his childhood.

## The Path to Islam

In 1977 that search was interrupted by a car accident that nearly killed him. During his recovery he began reading the Qur'an, and what he found there did not feel foreign. He recognised in Islam many of the concepts he had known as a Christian, and he later described his conversion as a "fulfillment" rather than a break. He became a Muslim that year — and then set about learning the religion properly, which is where his extraordinary path began.

## The Moment of Guidance

He was struck first by a young Saudi couple he met who followed the Scottish Sufi teacher Abdalqadir as-Sufi, and he moved to Norwich, England, to study under him directly. In 1979 he went to Al Ain in the United Arab Emirates, spending four years at the Islamic Studies Institute of UAE University, much of it studying one-to-one with scholars. He became fluent in Arabic and learned Qur'anic recitation, rhetoric, poetry, Maliki jurisprudence and theology. In 1984 he parted ways with as-Sufi and moved to North Africa — Algeria, Morocco, Spain, Mauritania — where he formed his most lasting bond with the Mauritanian scholar Murabit al-Hajj.

## Legacy

In 1996 Yusuf co-founded the Zaytuna Institute in Berkeley, California, with Zaid Shakir and Hatem Bazian, dedicated to reviving traditional methods of Islamic study. In 2010 it opened as Zaytuna College, a four-year Muslim liberal arts college — the first of its kind in the United States — and in 2015 it became the first accredited Muslim campus in the country. He completed a doctorate at the Graduate Theological Union in 2020, has been listed in the top fifty of The 500 Most Influential Muslims, and is one of the most recognised Muslim scholars in the West: praised as the "All-American Sheikh" and, at times, sharply criticised for his political positions. His life is a study in what happens when a young man's near-death question is answered by a Book.
